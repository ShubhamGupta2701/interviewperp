Computer Networks: TCP Congestion Control

Focus: How TCP adapts its sending rate to network congestion

1. Core Concepts

TCP congestion control prevents a sender from injecting data into the network faster than the network can handle it. The sender maintains a congestion window (cwnd), which limits how much unacknowledged data may be in flight.

  • cwnd: Sender-side congestion window.
  • rwnd: Receiver-advertised window used for flow control.
  • ssthresh: Slow-start threshold.
  • Slow Start: Rapidly increases the sending rate.
  • Congestion Avoidance: Increases the rate more cautiously.
  • Fast Retransmit: Retransmits after duplicate ACK evidence rather than waiting for timeout.
  • Fast Recovery: Attempts to recover from loss without returning completely to the initial sending rate.
Key relationship
Effective sending window ≈ min(cwnd, rwnd)

2. Important Rules & Formulas

Slow Start: cwnd grows approximately exponentially with each round-trip time (RTT), because acknowledgements cause cwnd to increase repeatedly during the RTT.

Approximate progression:
cwnd = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 ... MSS per RTT

Congestion Avoidance: cwnd grows approximately linearly—roughly one MSS per RTT in the classic model.

cwnd ≈ cwnd + 1 MSS per RTT

This behavior is commonly summarized as AIMD:

Additive Increase, Multiplicative Decrease.

3. Teaching Explanation

A useful classroom analogy is a highway. TCP does not initially know how much traffic the highway can support, so it starts cautiously and increases traffic quickly while acknowledgements indicate successful delivery.

Once the sender reaches the slow-start threshold, it becomes more conservative and enters congestion avoidance. If packet loss suggests congestion, TCP reduces its sending rate.

The important distinction for students is that flow control protects the receiver, while congestion control protects the network.

4. Examples

Example 1 — Slow Start

Suppose cwnd starts at 1 MSS. Ignoring implementation details, its approximate RTT-by-RTT growth is:

1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 MSS

This demonstrates exponential growth.

Example 2 — Entering Congestion Avoidance

If ssthresh = 8 MSS, TCP grows rapidly until cwnd reaches the threshold. Afterward, growth becomes approximately linear:

1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 9 → 10 → 11 ...

Example 3 — Receiver Limitation

If cwnd = 20 KB but the receiver advertises rwnd = 8 KB, the sender is limited to approximately:

min(20 KB, 8 KB) = 8 KB

5. Common Misconceptions

  • “Slow Start is slow.”
    Despite its name, its window growth is aggressive and approximately exponential.
  • “cwnd and rwnd are the same.”
    cwnd addresses network congestion; rwnd addresses receiver capacity.
  • “Every packet loss always means TCP timeout.”
    Duplicate acknowledgements can allow TCP to detect and retransmit a missing segment before the retransmission timer expires.

6. Exam & Interview Points

  • Be able to distinguish flow control from congestion control.
  • Know the roles of cwnd, rwnd, and ssthresh.
  • Remember: Slow Start ≈ exponential growth; Congestion Avoidance ≈ linear growth.
  • Understand AIMD.
  • Be prepared to calculate cwnd progression over several RTTs in simplified textbook problems.
  • Know why duplicate ACKs can provide evidence of packet loss before a timeout occurs.

7. Practice Questions

Q1. What is the primary difference between TCP flow control and congestion control?

Answer: Flow control prevents overwhelming the receiver; congestion control prevents overwhelming the network.

Q2. If cwnd begins at 1 MSS and remains in simplified Slow Start for four growth rounds, what sequence is observed?

Answer: 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 MSS.

Q3. If cwnd = 15 KB and rwnd = 10 KB, what limits the amount of unacknowledged data the sender can have in flight?

Answer: The receiver window; the effective limit is approximately 10 KB.

Professor's 60-second recap

TCP dynamically controls its transmission rate using cwnd. Slow Start discovers available capacity quickly, Congestion Avoidance increases the rate cautiously, and packet-loss signals cause TCP to reduce its rate. Keep congestion control (network protection) distinct from flow control (receiver protection).
